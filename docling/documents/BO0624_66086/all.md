- 91 A HENRY VII. MAZER BOWL-74 in. diam., 2 in. deep —London hall-mark,1507;makers mark,a feur-de-lys

The Almshouses were founded in the year 1400,and in the parchment boundOrdinances"of the Almshouses in the possession of the Trustees it is recorded in an English translation of the original Latin "That in ye y of Or Lord 1400 on y 5th day August The Parishioners and moreable inhabitantsofWalden by anunanimous consent

The mazer is formed as a shallow bowl, of polished maple-wood, with a slight foot of the wood beneath the centre;it is mounted with a plain silver-gilt rim,while in the centre of the bowl is a silver-gilt boss or medallion —24 in. diam.—engraved with The Virgin and Child, depicting the Virgin standing, wearing a crown and flowing robes and holding the Infant Saviour on her right arm, the two figures being shown on a rayed background and comon assistance ofywhole community of ysame, wholesomely providing for ycure and safety of their Souls did piously build certain houses to y Honor of God and his Glorious Mother to continue for y refuge and main-
tenance of 13poor people,wch houses are in a certain Lane called Daniels Lane"

The Mazer Bowl has remained in the possession of the Almshouses from the time ofits original gift until the present day,and it is of interest torecall that the Diarist Samuel Pepys,in describing his visit to Saffron Walden,in 1659,mentions the Mazer Bowl : "In our going,my landlord carried us through a very old hospital or almshouse,where forty poor people was maintained;a very old foundation and over the chimney in the mantelpiece was an inscription poor boc also was on the same chimneypiece,with an iron door and locks toit,into which I put 6d.They brought mea draftof their drink in abrownbowl,tiptwithsilver, which I drank off,and at thebottom was a picture of the Virgin and the Child in her arms, done in silver.So we went to onur inn(White Hart) and after eating of something, and kissed the daughter of the house,she berng very pretty, we took leave"

Further in the same document there is a list of gifts to the Almshouses at unspecified dates,and among the list under the heading of"Benefactors"appears the following: "A broad cup to drink in, price of 40 shill y wch Cupp Margaret Bregcheman gave to serve in y Foresd House perpetual for ye souls of her and of Stephen Bregcheman and all their friends,and in the margin is the word "Mazer"and "Margart. Stephen Bridgeman"

The Mazer Bowl is mentioned in an inventory of 154, where it is recorded that there was"in the Botre (butter!y) a masyr wt sylver and gylt"

The Mazer Bowl is mentioned in "The History of Audley End and Parish of Saffron Walden,by Richard

Lord Braybrooke,1836

In 1887 Mr.William Hope,writing in"Archeologia vol.l,states,"Of late years theBowl has been used for the Governors to drink out ofat the Annual Election and on some other occasions;the custom being that each person drinking out of it gave a shilling to the Town Crier who handed it round.It was so used quite recently,but being found ieaky the practice has been discontinued.Itis now used asa ballot dish and for dispensing certain money doles to the inmates of the Almshouses"
