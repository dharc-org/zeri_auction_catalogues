## HURSTMONCEUX.

THE remains of the castle of Hurstmonceux represent one of the finest domestic buildings of the time of Henry VI.,and the castle has the distinction of being the first great house built of brick south of the

The original builder of the castle was Sir Roger de Fienes, who was baptised "on the Feast of the Exaltation of the Cross"in 1384 at Hurstmonceux Church,where his father was laid to rest beneath a slab

Sir Rogerwas thirty-one years of age when,with his eight men-at-arms and twenty-four archers,he sailed with Henry V.from Southampton in 1415 on the expedition which lead to the surprising victory of Agincourt with a brass of his figure in full armour in 1405.

on Oetober 25.

The fight for the crown of France began to turn against the English after the death of Henry V. in 1422,but continued to the end of Sir Roger Fienes life, and during its earlier phases at all events must have been profitable to him or he would not have been able to contemplate the building of this most sumptuous castle covering an area of some 45,000 feet; but it was not until after Charles VII.had regained Paris that Sir Roger obtained his licentia kernellandithe necessary Royal permission to embattle his dwelling. This appears in the Patent Roll of 19 Henry VI.,that is in 1440,together with leave to enclose and enlarge his park so that it is afterwards described as being "three miles about."

The castle descended to Thomas,ffteenth Baron Dacre,of the time of Charles II.,whose extravagances obliged him to sell it,and after being bought by a Naylor and passing to a Hare it was disroofed and gutted in 1777.

The Revd. Edward Venables in his account of Hurstmonceux, outside walls remain almost complete and have suffered but little except in the loss of some portion of the battlements";then followed another seventy years of decay and neglect until the castle was purchased by the late Col. Claude Lowther who repaired the South Front,preserving and where necessary renovating its original features with great care,and then proceeded to furnish with considerable taste those portions of the building which he had once more made habitable.

The Castle was illustrated and described in “Country Life,”on

May 18,1929,and we are indebted to the article by Mr.H.Avray Tipping in that number for the foregoing historical notes.

Hurstmonceux Castle is situated about 7 miles from Eastbourne (via Pevensey), 4 miles from Hailsham,and about 3 miles from Pevensey.
