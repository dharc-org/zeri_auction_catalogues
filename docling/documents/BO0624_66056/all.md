Rossetti and Miss Browning were also present on this famous evening, which is vivaciously described by Mrs. Browning in a letter to Miss Mitford, written shortly afterwards:"One of the pleasantest things which has happened to us here is the coming down on us of the Laureate,who,being in London for three or four days from the Isle of Wight, spent two of them with us, dined with us, smoked with us, opened his heart to us (and the second bottle of port),and ended by reading 'Maud'through from end to end,and going away at half-past two in the morning. If I had had a heart to spare, certainly he would have won mine. He is captivating with his frankness,confidingness,and unexampled naivete! Think of his stopping inMaud'every now and then-'There's a wonderful touch! That's very tender. How beautiful that is!'Yes,and it was wonderful, tender,beautiful, and he read exquisitely in a voice like an organ, rather music than speech.
